Alp Inangu

I am a Research Assistant at Forschungszentrum Jülich (FZJ), IAS-6: Computational and Systems Neuroscience, where I work with the groups of Prof. Abigail Morrison and Prof. Markus Diesmann. I am also an undergraduate student in Computer Engineering at RWTH Aachen University.

My research interests lie at the intersection of computational neuroscience, machine learning, and high-performance computing. I am particularly interested in how the structure and dynamics of biological neural networks give rise to learning and information processing, and how these principles can inform the design of more adaptable and computationally efficient learning algorithms and computing systems.

My current research involves developing infrastructure for closed-loop neural simulations, investigating the performance and scalability of the NEST simulator on high-performance computing systems, and exploring how biologically grounded cortical circuit models can improve the cross-session generalization of brain–computer interface decoders.

NEST Simulator

NEST is an open-source simulator for large-scale spiking neural networks. My work focuses on performance analysis and hardware-aware optimization of neural simulations on conventional supercomputers, including JUPITER, with the aim of advancing performance baselines for neuromorphic computing.

NUMA balancing hampering performance of spiking network simulations
Melissa Lober, Alp Inangu, Gorka Peraza Coppola, Dennis Terhorst, Sebastian Gillessen, Jan Vogelsang, Hans Ekkehard Plesser, Brian Wylie, Benedikt Steinbusch, Guido Trensch, Susanne Kunkel, Markus Diesmann
arXiv, 2026
arXiv / paper

We investigate how the interaction between Linux's automatic NUMA balancing and memory allocation affects the performance of distributed spiking neural network simulations. Our results show that disabling automatic NUMA balancing can reduce energy consumption by approximately 30%, leading to the introduction of a user-configurable Slurm option at the Jülich Supercomputing Centre.

NEST Interact
Research Software · 2026
Upcoming release

A middleware framework for coupling spiking neural network simulators with external environments, enabling closed-loop experiments. Building on the MUSIC runtime, I redesigned its architecture around a standalone C++ library with Python bindings, and developed an encoder–decoder interface that simplifies signal exchange between neural agents and their environments.
